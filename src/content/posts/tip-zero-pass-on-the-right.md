## Your feelings are the reason marketing departments exist

Nobody has ever closed a deal with a spec sheet. As any good salesman
knows, emotional manipulation beats charts and graphs. Every time. When
people ask for car buying advice, what they're really after is
validation. They want to feel like less of a prat for buying a Jeep
Wrangler. Any commission-starved sycophant is only too happy to oblige
such a customer.

Did you know that 87% of passenger vehicles have a 5-star safety rating
as tested by the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration? You
actually have to try quite hard to find a car below this standard. Which
begs the question: What kind of halfwit would choose such a
conspicuously unsafe vehicle?

On an unrelated note, the Jeep Wrangler improved its safety rating to
4-stars in 2019.

## Psychology is just Biology

People are not rational decision makers. Coming to this realization is a
prerequisite to understanding human behavior. Our brains crave
coherence. Hearing someone say "*I liked the color*" is not a socially
acceptable answer that conveys intelligence in the car buying decision.
People will construct a retrospective narrative to scaffold their
impulsive decisions. Because of course it was the result of a deliberate
logical process.

On cue, the good salesman offers this validation: You picked a handsome
color. *Dopamine release*. And wow, he says we're so forward-thinking on
choosing the hybrid option! *More dopamine*. Why not add paint
protection for our new vehicle? All the savvy customers do it.
*Dopamine - I want to be savvy - dopamine*. And we must be so busy at
our important job, think of the hassle we would save by prepaying for a
maintenance plan and extended warranty! *Wow, I have an important job!
My parents must be proud of me!*

Thus dopamine wins over data. I could present a carefully constructed
argument that involves an amortization table. That has to compete with
the warm fuzzies of proud parents. You don't make many friends pulling
the brakes on the dopamine train. That was the sound of Bubba signing an
8% car note on a new Jeep Wrangler.

## They're in the way (Ugh, traffic)

Do you realize these people get to vote? Worse still, they get to drive.
If you think other people suck at driving, you are correct. Although not
in the way you expect: 80% of people think they are better drivers than
average. Ponder that numerical impossibility for a moment. Mandatory
driverless vehicles cannot come soon enough.

It is apparent that nobody in America understands the concept of a
passing lane. Given what we know about people's feelings - *"Nobody
should drive faster than I'm comfortable with" -* what is one to do? I
suggest passing on the right**.** Not because this will lead to some
great revolution in driver's education - it won't. It's just mildly
satisfying.

## Lies, damned lies, and Santa Claus

But let's return to Bubba - our intrepid protagonist who's the proud
owner of a brand-new lime-green Jeep Wrangler hybrid with an extended
warranty. Gosh, I'm sure his parents are proud of him. You see, people
can be dishonest with their friends, their family, their therapist, and
even with themselves. But nobody has yet figured out how to lie to
almighty Google.

After all, the only way to get what you want out of a search engine is
to be explicitly honest with your query. If you're too embarrassed to
ask how to properly transplant a hydrangea, and instead Google the
method of growing from seed, you'll never master gardening. That
sentence was not about gardening. Bubba would rather keep his search
history private, thank you very much.

Yet for the sake of society - we lie. It starts with the little lies of
Santa Claus, so children learn to believe. How else can they grow into
adults who trust in the big lies of justice, mercy, and the value of
money? The same money that only works in-so-far as there is a massive,
coordinated social lie: everyone agrees to act as if it has value.

## Know your worth - it might be less than you think

We're not ready for a society with no concept of money. We measure
everything from attorney fees to petrol to zygote development in
dollars. Can you believe the cost of eating out these days? Which brings
us back to Bubba, our waiter with the new Jeep Wrangler in the parking
lot. His fake smile and impossibly cheery voice being calculated
business decisions to entice the customer into ordering more drinks.
He's feverishly behind on tips because the Becky in the blouse with the
top button undone seems to get all the attention.

Only in the most culinary bankrupt of establishments is the disingenuous
flirtatiousness of the server adding value to my meal. I'd rather get my
food on a conveyer belt. The most grotesque lie in America is that
people tip for good service. If there is one practice that is
universally repulsive to the rest of the world, it is the socially
coerced 20%. Let's kindly stop pretending that tipping is anything but
discrimination against ugly people and bad weather.

## Being Right vs Being Effective

There's a prevailing philosophy in business that success requires
compromise - that a skilled leader knows how to empathize with others to
achieve a desired outcome. I say bollocks to that. The world is moving
in a direction where being right ***is*** being effective. The ability
to reliably predict the future means people *are* profits.

Is there another reason why 200,000 Jeep Wranglers are sold every year?
